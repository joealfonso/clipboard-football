# MODEL.md

How the play engine decides outcomes. Every part is marked **fitted** (estimated from nflverse data), **designed** (set by hand for the game), or **mixed** (designed structure with fitted numbers). The offense vs defense matchup table is designed, never data-derived.

## Status labels

Each section below states fitted, designed or mixed, and what data or reasoning backs it.

## Play engine overview

Built in M1 step 4 (docs/specs/m1-step4-play-engine.md). `createEngine(data)` returns `resolvePlay(state, offenseCall, defenseCall, players, rng)`, the only function that sees both calls (packages/sim/src/engine/resolve.ts). For a run or pass it: looks up the fitted class probabilities for the play's leaf in this situation; multiplies them by the matchup, formation and rating multipliers and renormalizes within the family; draws a class; draws one real play of that class from its pool with its season weight; applies that play's yards at the current spot; draws fouls at fitted rates and pairs them with a physical play; draws the seconds the play used. Every draw uses the Rng passed in, in a fixed order. The result is a step 3 PlayResult; `detail` carries the trace, the why sentence, the class probabilities and the foul rates used. Rebuilt by `uv run --project pipeline python -m pipeline.engine`; results in docs/ENGINE_REPORT.md.

## Matchup table

**Designed, never data-derived.** data/engine/matchup.json holds raw multipliers on the baseline class probabilities for offense leaf x defensive preset x class; every cell not listed is 1. Bounds 0.5 to 2.0. Every notable cell (outside 0.9-1.1) carries the label `designed` and a one-line football reason in the file. No cell is `mixed` in this version: none was set from a participation-data comparison, so none claims a data source. The main shapes:

- Runs against Run Stuff and Goal Line: more stuffs (x1.25, x1.3), fewer second-level runs, but more 20+ runs (one or no deep defender behind a loaded box).
- Runs against light boxes (Dime Cover 4, Dime Cover 0 Blitz, Prevent): fewer stuffs, more 4-9 and 10-19 runs.
- Passes against blitzes (Nickel Cover 1 Blitz, Dime Cover 0 Blitz): more sacks (x1.35 to x1.6) and interceptions; screens against them gain more 10+ and 20+ (x1.4, x1.5) and take fewer sacks.
- Deep passes: Cover 0 gives up big completions (x1.35), two-high and quarters shells cap them (x0.85, x0.8), Prevent takes them away (x0.6).
- Intermediate passes find the Cover 2 hole (x1.15); quarters sits on them (x0.88).
- Short passes and screens do better against soft zones (Dime Cover 4, Prevent) and worse against tight man.
- Play action burns run-first defenses (x1.3 on 20+ against Run Stuff, Goal Line, Base Cover 3) and does nothing against six-DB packages (x0.8).

**Formation layer (designed, spec resolved question 12).** A small formation x defensive package layer in the same file: Heavy helps short-yardage inside runs and sneaks (more 1-3 yard gains, fewer stuffs) and helps against light packages; Shotgun and Empty get quick throws out (short and screen passes complete more) and cost inside and outside runs a little; Empty raises sacks, most against Dime, the blitz package. Formation also matters through the fitted conditioning: the finest baseline level is keyed by formation (74 stored cells), so shotgun and under-center runs differ where the data supports it. Both effects are visible in play: on 3rd and 1, an inside run from Heavy gains 1-3 yards 48.8% of the time against 44.4% from Shotgun and is stuffed 17.5% against 22.2%; a short pass from Empty against Dime Cover 4 on 3rd and 8 is a sack 15.1% of the time against 12.5% from Shotgun.

**Centering.** The engine reads data/engine/matchup.centered.json, which only the pipeline's normalization writes (pipeline/engine/center.py); the raw file is never touched. Constraint: for each leaf, over the 2016-2023 training plays (season weight x frac, real formation), with each preset weighted by the step 2 defense caller's shares in that play's situation, the class probabilities after the multipliers equal the baseline's. Method: an iterative per-leaf, per-class correction factor (proportional fitting), first for the formation layer alone, then for the matchup table with the centered formation layer applied, to within 1e-9. The raw multipliers shift the leaf aggregates by up to 1.7 percentage points; the centered ones by 0.000 (ENGINE_REPORT.md section 8). What it means for play: a human who picks the right defense beats the average, and the average AI defense gives real NFL numbers. Two-point tries use the same table; they are not part of the centering sample, so they are close to centered, not exact.

## Play outcome models

**Fitted, with designed classes and bins.** Source: regular-season pbp 2016-2023, season-weighted with a 2-season half-life anchored at 2023; never 2024, 2025 or 2026 (a pytest feeds those seasons and shows they never reach a fit). Plays with an accepted foul are left out of the pools (fouls are drawn separately), so a foul-prone play type (holding on long runs) is slightly under-represented; that bias is accepted.

- Families and leaves: each real play is mapped to a step 2 leaf by the step 2 mapping code itself (pipeline/caller/mapping.py, reused through pipeline.caller.load.build_season). Run leaves 2016-2023. Pass leaves 2022-2023 only: screen and play action need FTN charting, which starts in 2022 (the same seasons step 2 uses for pass type and depth).
- Unknown-depth dropbacks (sacks, scrambles, most throwaways) have no depth leaf. Each is split across the short, intermediate and deep leaves by the step 2 caller's depth shares in its situation (fractional weight; spec resolved question 13), so those three leaves get the same sack and scramble rate in a given situation before matchups. Training sack rates: short 7.2%, intermediate 8.0%, deep 8.0%, play action 7.0%, screen 0.15%.
- Classes (designed, data/engine/config.json): run: fumble, loss or no gain, 1-3, 4-9, 10-19, 20+; pass: sack, scramble, interception, incomplete, fumble after catch, completion for 0 or less, 1-9, 10-19, 20+. Every pooled play lands in exactly one.
- Situation cells: backoff (leaf, formation, down, dist5, zone6, score3, time2), then without formation, then (leaf, down, dist3, zone6), (leaf, dist3, zone3), (leaf, zone3), (leaf), family. A cell is stored with at least 300 plays.
- Within a class, a whole real play is drawn, untouched: yards, out of bounds, fumble recovery, interception spot and return. Pools are keyed (leaf, class, 10-yard band), backing off to (leaf, class, zone3), (leaf, class), (family, class); a pool is stored with at least 100 plays, and the last level always. Yards are applied at the current spot: reaching the goal line is a touchdown, a loss into the own end zone a safety. A real play that scored is a touchdown where it is applied only when it was drawn from that spot's own band (5 yards or fewer inside the 10); from a coarser backoff pool it scores only if its yards reach the goal line (post-validation revision 8: before, any play that scored was a touchdown anywhere in a 10-yard band). Cells split the red zone at the 10 and 15 and the own 10 at the 5 (`zone7`); pools use 2- to 5-yard bands inside both 20s (`ydlP`). A kneel never carries the ball into the own end zone, and a pooled punt fielded or downed past the goal line is a touchback (revisions 12, 13). Interceptions and lost fumbles are rebuilt from the turnover spot (air yards, or yards to the fumble) and the return.
- Kneels and spikes: real yards and seconds, no classes or multipliers.
- Two-point tries: whole real two-point plays per leaf; a leaf with fewer than 300 backs off to scrimmage plays snapped at the 2, then at the goal line (zone6 GL), then to every try of the family. Success is the ball reaching the end zone. The fourth level, tryFamily, is a last resort and is used below the minimum: intermediate, deep and screen passes have no 300-play source (deep and intermediate depth cannot happen at the goal line), so they take the 96 pass tries with a charted leaf (2022-2023), the same pool for all three. Their two-point outcomes do not differ by leaf.
- Fluke flag (designed): the drawn class had baseline probability under 5%, or the play's yards beat the cell's 97.5th percentile.

## Special teams

**Punts (fitted).** Whole real punts 2016-2023 by line-of-scrimmage band. Classes: blocked, touchback, muffed, return touchdown, and fair catch, downed, out of bounds and returned, each split at 45 gross yards (so the punter's leg has a named class to move). The real gross distance and return are applied at the current spot; a blocked punt ends 8 yards behind the line (designed), recovered by the side the real play shows.

**Field goals (fitted, half-life 1 season).** Make probability among unblocked kicks is a weighted logistic curve in distance and distance squared; block rate by distance band; a blocked kick's recovery and return come from the real blocked kicks. Beyond the longest distance with at least 20 attempts (59 yards) the probability is capped there and falls linearly to 0 at 70 yards (designed, spec resolved question 6). The kick spot is 8 yards behind the line, the most common measured depth. Accuracy has risen every season; ENGINE_REPORT.md section 5 shows the curve against each training season. Pre-registered expectation: 2024-2025 accuracy, especially 50+, sits above the curve (it did: 50+ predicted 63.8%, actual 69.5%, within the 6-point tolerance).

**Extra points (fitted).** Good, missed and blocked rates, 2016-2023, 2-season half-life. Holdout accuracy is higher than the fit (see Calibration).

**Kickoffs: designed, tuned to the 2025 season.** The format changed in 2023, 2024 and 2025 (see docs/DATA.md and docs/DECISIONS.md), so no training season matches current rules. data/engine/kickoff.json holds outcome weights (touchback in the end zone or after the landing zone, short, out of bounds, fair catch, returned, return touchdown, kickers recover), the return-end spot distribution and the designed fouls, tuned by hand until simulated kickoffs matched 2025: touchbacks 21.0% vs 21.0%, returns 76.9% vs 75.7%, out of bounds 1.4% vs 1.4%, mean start 30.7 vs 30.3. Because 2025 is also a holdout season, this check confirms the tuning rather than testing a prediction. This is the one exception to the holdout rule. The 2026 changes: a touchback from a kick at the 50 goes to the 20 (6-1-5), which the machine applies and the engine's distribution does not need to know; onside kicks may be declared any time (6-1-6), which changes when teams try them (a calling decision, step 5 or M3), not the recovery rate of a declared onside kick. That rate is the 2025 share, 5 of 52 (9.6%, interval 4.2-20.6%), few kicks, so it is designed. Safety kicks are rare; their outcomes are designed and marked so.

The defense call does not affect punts or field goals in M1 (spec resolved question 3): block rates are the fitted baseline only.

## Penalties

**Fitted rates, designed pairing.** Rates per snap, 2016-2023, by family (run, pass, punt, field goal, extra point) and down x distance band, with backoff to the family; accepted and declined fouls both count, from every foul named in the play description (the machine decides acceptance). Pre-snap fouls are fitted on run and pass snaps together (a pre-snap foul has no play to classify) and drawn before the snap. The mapping from pbp's penalty type to a rule set id is designed (config.json): Neutral Zone Infraction counts as encroachment; pbp's one Unnecessary Roughness type is split into the live-ball foul (when the down was wiped out) and the dead-ball foul (when the play stood); a receiving-team block above the waist on a punt is the return-team illegal block. Every real type 2016-2025 is mapped to an id or to "unsupported"; an unmapped type fails the build.

Each snap is fitted into one category: no supported foul, one team's foul (named by its first supported foul), or fouls by both teams. So the engine, which draws one category per snap, reproduces the share of snaps with a flag; it never emits two fouls by the same team. Known gap, no model change: real snaps carry two or more supported fouls 0.18% of the time in-sample (0.21% in 2024-2025) against the engine's 0.07%, which is its both-teams pairs only (`flags.twoOrMore` in ENGINE_REPORT.md section 1); the machine enforces one foul per team anyway, so the missing second foul changes the flag count, not the yardage. The live-foul draw happens only on snaps with no pre-snap foul, so its uniform is scaled to the remaining mass and each live category comes out at its fitted per-snap rate. detail.foulRates gives each id's probability of being on the snap (its one-team rate plus its share of both-teams pairs), plus anyFlag and twoFlags.

Pairing (designed): pre-snap fouls replace the play with a noPlay; defensive pass interference, offensive pass interference and intentional grounding pair with an incompletion (interference at a spot drawn from the fitted distribution of enforced interference yards; grounding 7 yards behind the line), and the play as enforced is that incompletion: its class, yards, trace, fluke check and why sentence describe the incompletion, not the discarded draw (detail.sampledClass and sampledYards keep the draw, which the validation compares with real plays without an accepted foul); the rest attach to a play sampled normally. Fouls on plays with a change of possession and on kicks come from one team only; fouls by both teams (0.144% of run and pass snaps) only on run and pass plays without a change of possession. The dead-ball personal foul is emitted only when the ball is dead in the field of play.

Supported ids (15): the 12 of step 3 plus offensive pass interference, illegal contact and live-ball unnecessary roughness (spec resolved question 5, cited in RULES.md). Remaining gap: real fouls the rule set does not support account for about 19.8 enforced yards per game in 2016-2023 (18.7 in 2024-2025), against about 82 for supported fouls; face masks, unsportsmanlike conduct, illegal use of hands, illegal formation and shift, ineligible man downfield and taunting are the largest (ENGINE_REPORT.md section 6). Known gap: pre-snap fouls on extra points are counted with run and pass snaps, so the engine never calls one on an extra point.

## Ratings

**Mixed: measured inputs, designed mapping and effects.** Every player has Speed, Strength, Agility and Awareness plus position skills, integers 0-99 (packages/shared/src/engine.ts; zod schema in engine-schema.ts, schemaVersion 1):

| Position | Skills |
|---|---|
| QB | Throw Power, Accuracy |
| RB | Ball Security, Elusiveness |
| WR | Hands, Route Running |
| TE | Hands, Route Running, Run Block |
| OL | Pass Block, Run Block |
| DL | Pass Rush, Run Stop |
| LB | Tackling, Coverage |
| CB, S | Coverage, Ball Skills |
| K, P | Kick Power, Kick Accuracy |

- Measured (mixed): Speed from the 40; Strength from bench reps (weight 0.75) and body weight (0.25); Agility from the 3-cone and shuttle (0.5 each). Each drill's percentile is computed within position over drafted players of draft classes 2010-2023 who ran it, then mapped through a designed curve (percentile 0, 10, 50, 90, 100 to rating 30, 48, 65, 82, 99). A drill with fewer than 20 players at a position is not used there (QB bench, K and P drills), and the rating is designed for that position. Ratings are therefore position-relative: an offensive lineman's Speed of 80 means fast for a lineman. Only per-position percentile cut points ship (data/players/distributions.json).
- Designed: Awareness and every position skill, drawn from the same curve.
- Generator (M1): `generateTeam(rng, data)` fills every slot any formation, package or kick unit needs, draws each rating from the curve, and names from data/players/names.json (fictional; the pipeline checks every first-last pair against nflverse rosters from 1999 on and found 0 matches).
- Edges and effects (designed, data/players/ratings.json): 31 effects. Each multiplies a named class or penalty rate by 1 + sum of coef x (the on-field players' average rating - the position's league mean), clamped. For a class effect the multiplier is m + q(m - 1)^2, where q is the class's baseline share, which cancels the small downward drift that renormalizing would otherwise give a spread of teams. The edge is relative to the same slots' league average, so a league-average unit gives exactly 1 and package differences (Dime is light against the run) live in the matchup table only. Effects compose multiplicatively with the matchup and formation multipliers.
- What each rating does (D6: one player raised from his position's 10th to 90th percentile, all else league average): QB Speed and Agility raise scrambles (+39%, +20%), Awareness cuts grounding (-51%) and sacks, Throw Power raises deep completions (+26%), Accuracy cuts interceptions (-27%), Strength wins sneaks; RB Speed and Elusiveness raise 20+ runs (+40%, +26%), Agility cuts stuffs, Strength wins short yardage, Awareness cuts sacks (blitz pickup), Ball Security cuts fumbles (-68%); WR and TE Speed raise 20+ completions, Agility raises yards after catch, Hands and Route Running cut incompletions, WR Strength cuts interceptions, Awareness cuts false starts and offensive interference, TE Run Block and Strength cut stuffs; OL Pass Block cuts sacks, Run Block and Strength cut stuffs, Agility cuts holding, Awareness cuts false starts, Speed beats screen stuffs; DL Pass Rush, Speed and Agility raise sacks, Run Stop and Strength raise stuffs, Awareness cuts offside; LB Speed cuts 20+ runs, Strength raises stuffs, Agility stops screens, Awareness stops play action, Tackling forces fumbles, Coverage raises incompletions; CB and S Speed cut 20+ completions, Agility cuts yards after catch, Strength cuts 20+ runs, Awareness cuts interference and illegal contact, Coverage raises incompletions, Ball Skills raises interceptions; K Kick Power raises end-zone touchbacks, Kick Accuracy cuts misses, and the K and P base ratings cut return touchdowns (the last line of defense); P Kick Power lengthens punts, Kick Accuracy (hang time) cuts returns. The four ratings the spec table did not give an effect (QB Strength, OL Speed, DL Agility, LB Agility) got one so the fun rule holds; the weakest are OL Speed (2.6%), DL Agility (3.3%), OL Agility (3.4%) and OL Strength (3.7%), one lineman of five. The minimum is 2% (designed). Full numbers: the D6 test in packages/sim/test/engine/effects.test.ts.

## Why sentence

**Designed.** Each play records a structured trace (detail.trace): the call, the cell and backoff level, the class drawn with its baseline and final probability, the matchup and formation multipliers on that class, each rating effect on it, the fouls, the special-teams event, and the fluke flag. The sentence (detail.why) comes from a template in data/engine/why.json whose slots bind only to trace fields; a slot with no value throws, there is no fallback text. Order: a pre-snap foul, then a fluke, then a foul on the play, then the dominant factor (largest absolute log multiplier on the class that happened, ties by matchup, rating, formation; under 0.05 none dominates and the sentence says it was a common result). Kicks have their own templates.

## Clock and tempo

The clock rules are **cited NFL rules**, neither fitted nor designed: when the clock stops, when it restarts, the play clock, the two-minute warning, the ten-second run-off and untimed downs all come from data/rules/nfl-2026.json, each value cited in docs/RULES.md from the 2026 rulebook (built in M1 step 3). A few simplifications of those rules are designed and listed in RULES.md.

**Tempo, timeouts and onside kicks (mixed, M1 step 5).** Designed structure (data/game/config.json: the dead-ball reading of pbp, the clock-running rule, bins, backoff levels, N = 150), fitted numbers (data/game/tempo.json, timeouts.json, onside.json, built by `uv run --project pipeline python -m pipeline.game`; docs/COACH_REPORT.md). Source check: nflverse `play_clock` is "0" on every row 2016-2025, so it cannot be used (DECISIONS.md). Timeout rows are read in clock order (designed, step 5c revision 1): a timeout belongs to the snap whose game clock it carries, with the time of day as the tie-break, and never carries across a period end; before it, 2022-2023 rows listed after their snap put the timeout one dead ball late.

- Tempo: seconds the offense lets run before the snap when the clock runs, or before a timeout called while it runs (the clock runs first; step 5 revision 1). Measured on 2022-2023 only (the seasons with play timestamps): game clock at the previous snap, minus that play's measured duration, minus the game clock at this snap; an empirical distribution of whole seconds 0-40 per cell. Bins: time5, the final-2:00 clock, score3, offense timeouts, previous snap a kneel, previous snap out of bounds or flagged. A draw above the play clock is clipped to it.
- Timeouts: probability that a team calls one in a dead ball before a scrimmage snap, as offense or defense, 2016-2023; bins side, time5, final-2:00 clock, score3 from the team's view, clock running, its timeouts left, 4th down. Masked to 0 when the team has none or already took one in the dead ball. Challenge and replay timeouts excluded. Before a kickoff or a try, fitted rates per role in designed time bins (step 5c milestone D, below). Step 5b (designed levels, fitted rates): inside the late windows (G5, GF and all of overtime, `lateT`) every level before the side-only fallback keeps score3; outside them every rate is unchanged.
- Onside: attempt probability per kickoff, 2016-2023, by quarter group, time left (final 2:00 or 5:00 of the 4th) and the kicker's score deficit, backing off through coarser deficit and time bins while a cell has 150 kickoffs; only below that the designed rule (trailing by 9 or more in the final 5:00 of the 4th or overtime). Recovery is the step 4 designed kickoff model. The fit reflects the older kickoff rules; 2026 lets an onside kick be declared any time.
- Ten-second run-off (designed): the offense spends a timeout instead when trailing or tied in the final 2:00 of a half with one left.
- Component validation against 2024-2025 (C7, pre-registered tolerances): all 11 metrics pass (docs/COACH_REPORT.md).

**Seconds a play uses (fitted, step 4).** nflverse pbp has no duration column, but from 2022 on `time_of_day` (the snap) and `end_clock_time` (the end of the play) are both timestamps, so their difference is the play's length (spec resolved question 4: a direct field exists, so the clean-subsample model was not needed). On incompletions, where the clock stops at the end of the play and starts on the snap, it matches the game clock change to the next snap within 1.5 seconds 89-91% of the time (median error 0.1-0.2 s). The engine draws whole seconds from a 2022-2023 histogram per family and class (and per punt and field goal class). Bias: the measured subsample is 94% of 2022-2023 runs and 93% of passes, with a class mix within 0.5 points of all plays; runs from 2016-2021 have no timestamps and take their class's histogram. Pre-snap run-off stays step 5's.

## Game rules defaults

**Designed.** The state machine (packages/sim/src/game) applies rules and decides no outcomes, but a few choices belong to the coaches. Until the UI (M2) and the AI coaches (M3) pass them in, these designed default policies decide, deterministically:

- Coin toss: the winner defers in regulation and receives in overtime. Whichever captain has the choice of kicking or receiving receives.
- Penalties: the offended team accepts unless declining leaves it with a score, possession, or a better down and distance, compared by down first, then distance, then spot. In full games (M1 step 5, post-validation revision 11) the loop passes each offended team's own choice instead: the option with the higher lead plus fitted expected points of the next snap (mixed: designed rule, nflverse `ep` fitted by down, distance and field position, data/game/ep.json).
- Ten-second run-off: the offense takes the run-off rather than spend a timeout.
- Untimed down: always taken when earned.

## Baseline play caller

**Mixed.** The shares are fitted from nflverse data. Everything that decides what gets fitted, and how a real play is read, is designed. Built in M1 step 2 (docs/specs/m1-step2-call-menus-and-baseline-caller.md); rebuilt by `uv run --project pipeline python -m pipeline.caller`; results in docs/CALLER_REPORT.md.

What it does: for a situation (down, distance, field position, score, clock, timeouts), it looks up how often real NFL teams made each call in that kind of situation and picks one at random with those odds, using the game's seeded RNG. The share functions (`offenseShares`, `defenseShares`, `formationShares` in packages/sim/src/caller) return the full distribution before any draw, so M3 personalities can tilt it. `chooseOffenseCall` and `chooseDefenseCall` then draw once for the call and, for a run or pass, once more for the formation. The defense caller takes only the situation; it never sees the offense's call, formation or personnel.

Fitted (from data):

- The integer weights in every cell of data/caller/offense.table.json and defense.table.json: shares of each call per situation cell, season-weighted, rounded to sum to 10,000.
- Seasons: offense 2016-2023, with the 4th-down go-or-kick decision 2021-2023 only (punt or field goal given a kick uses 2016-2023), pass type and pass depth 2022-2023 only; defense 2018-2023. Never 2024, 2025 or 2026.

Designed (by hand, never fitted, never data-derived):

- The catalog of calls and menus (data/calls/catalog.json) and its availability rules.
- The situation bins, the backoff chains, the minimum of 150 plays per stored cell and the 2-season half-life (data/caller/config.json).
- Every mapping from a real play to a call: the offense rules (pass, run, kick, clock, screen over play action, depth by air yards, inside or outside run, the QB sneak rule), the formation value table, the personnel token table, the coverage value table and the defense preset table.
- The 13 defensive presets and the rule that maps a real front and coverage onto them. This mapping is a design choice. It is not derived from data, and the mismatch numbers in CALLER_REPORT.md section 6 show where real calls do not fit it.
- The defense factorization: P(preset) = sum over fronts of P(front | situation) x P(coverage | front, coarse situation), mapped through the preset table. Its assumption: given the front and the situation, coverage on run plays looks like coverage on pass plays. Coverage is charted only on dropbacks (CALLER_REPORT.md section 3: about 89% of dropbacks 2018-2022, 99.6% from 2023, under 1% of runs), so the data cannot test this. The planned partial check, FTN blitz rate on runs vs dropbacks, turned out uninformative: FTN records zero blitzers on every run in 2022-2023.
- The two-point try model: one cell from two-point tries, backing off to goal-line plays when a cell has fewer than 150 tries.
- Tolerances for the caller's own validation (data/caller/tolerances.json).

Validation (2024 and 2025, never trained on): the latest result is in CALLER_REPORT.md section 9 and data/caller/validation.json. The first run failed 11 of 56 gated metrics, mostly 4th-down rates, because the backoff merged field goal range with punt range. Post-validation revision 1 (DECISIONS.md) made after seeing those results:

- split 4th down into go-or-kick (2021-2023, fine field zone dropped last) and punt-or-field-goal given a kick (2016-2023);
- kept the end-of-game clock state longer for downs 1-3;
- added Nickel Cover 2.

Post-validation revision 2 (step 5a, docs/specs/m1-step5a-caller-late-game-bins.md), added after the step 5 in-sample run: in the last 5:00 of the game the score bins of oTop, o4Top and o4Kick split trailing and leading by 1-3 from 4-8 (the bins and the designed kick zone `zoneK` are designed; the shares in the late cells are fitted, o4Top's late cells on 2016-2023 where 2021-2023 has fewer than 150 plays). Outside the last 5:00 nothing changed. Its 2024-2025 re-validation is post hoc.

Post-validation revision 3 (step 5b, docs/specs/m1-step5b-late-game-and-clock-control.md; config version 3). **Designed bins and level order, fitted shares.** Late levels in oTop and o4Top only:
- `lateTime` (designed): G5 (game 5:00 to 2:01), GF (final 2:00) and OT (all of overtime). Overtime cells are fitted on 2016-2023 overtime plays, which enter only levels keyed by `lateTime`; every other factor and level still bins overtime as the second half and never sees an overtime row.
- `scoreLate5Only` (designed): five late score buckets (<=-9, -8..-1, 0, 1..8, >=9), a union of the 5a late buckets that keeps the sign.
- `kneelOut` (designed, oTop): for a leading offense on downs 1-3 in GF or OT, K1 when (4 - d) x P + 2 - P x min(defense timeouts, 4 - d) >= seconds left, with P the rule set's play clock and 2 a designed allowance.
- Late levels pair the late score with the kick zone (`zoneK`), then field half. The pre-5b levels use regulation-only copies of their time and late-score bins (`time5R`, `time2R`, `scoreLateOnlyR`; same labels and keys, null in overtime), so overtime reaches its own cells first. Overtime without its own cell backs off to the G5/GF cells (`lateClock`: overtime with 2:00 or less reads GF, longer reads G5), whose levels are fitted on regulation rows only (lead's resolved question 2).
- Outside the late windows every share is unchanged (frozen-golden test). The level order is in DECISIONS.md. Its 2024-2025 re-validation is post hoc.

Step 5c milestone B (docs/specs/m1-step5c-playtest-fixes.md). **Field goal range: a designed mask and a designed bin, fitted shares.**
- Make-chance mask (designed, Resolved 3): the game's caller drops a field goal try wherever the engine's base make chance is 0, and every other call keeps its relative fitted weight. The zero is the step 4 designed rule in data/engine/baseline.special.json `fieldGoals` (the fitted curve capped at `capAt`, 59 yards, then linear to 0 at `maxDistance`, 70); the caller reads those values through `fieldGoalMakeChance` (packages/sim/src/caller/range.ts, shared with resolvePlay) and holds no limit of its own. The fitted tables and their Python mirror are unmasked; only the game applies the mask.
- `zoneFG` (designed, o4Kick only): zone6 with MID (opp 36-50, tries of 54-68 yards) split at the opp 41/42, the edge of the fitted make curve (`capAt`). Its levels come before each zone6 level, which stays as the fallback, so every other zone keeps its cells. The punt or field goal share in each cell is fitted, 2016-2023.

Step 5c milestone C. **Late try chart (designed, Resolved 2).** In the final 5:00 of Q4 no try cell (score after the touchdown x window) has 150 real tries in 2016-2023, so none is fitted. data/caller/config.json `tryChart` holds a narrow designed chart keyed by `tryLate` (G5: 5:00-2:01; GF: 2:00 or less; overtime reads the 4th-quarter cell with the same clock) and the score after the touchdown (`diff`). A cell is charted only where 2016-2023 shows one choice on at least 90% of 10 or more tries, and the chart then makes that choice outright: go for two at -5, -2 and +1 (and +4, +5 in GF; +5 in G5), kick everywhere else that qualifies. Every other late cell keeps the fitted oTry backoff, and outside the final 5:00 the shares are unchanged. A try replayed after a foul is asked again and gets the same rule. GAME_REPORT.md (C4) lists sim and real counts next to each cell.

**Late clock use (step 5c milestone E).** Fitted: two timeout levels first keep a five-way late score (`lateS5`: trailing by 9 or more, by 1-8, tied, leading by 1-8, by 9 or more; null outside G5, GF and overtime, so every other rate is unchanged). Designed (data/game/config.json `clockRules`): (1) clock-out: an offense trailing by 1-8 with a timeout left calls it rather than let a drawn run-off end the 4th quarter or overtime (the clock runs until it is called, from the timeout cells); evidence, 1 of 2,095 real games ends Q4 with such a team's last snap at 15 s or more. A tied team is not covered (letting it run to overtime is a real choice). (2) The run-off a team lets go before its own timeout stops at least 1 s short of the end of the half or the game (`timeoutBeforeExpiry`), since a timeout is called before the clock expires. (3) The average coach's caller never spikes with the clock stopped (real: 6 such spikes in 2,095 games); the caller sees only whether the clock runs.

**Timeouts before kickoffs and tries (mixed, step 5c milestone D).** Fitted per role (kicking, receiving, scoring, other team) in designed time bins (`kickTime`: Q1-Q3, Q4 before 5:00, 5:00-2:01, the final 2:00; data/game/config.json `specialTimeouts`), read from the kick view, backing off to the role's pooled rate (N = 150).

## Home field

**Designed, tuned on 2016-2023 only.** data/game/league.json. Centered and symmetric: every home player's ratings get +0.69 points and every away player's -0.69 for the game, so league totals over home and away together stay at the baseline, and offensive pre-snap foul rates (false start, delay of game) are x 0.969 for the home offense and x 1.031 for the away offense. The crowd-noise factor 0.031 is measured (2016-2023 without 2020, non-neutral games: 1.77% of home-offense snaps against 1.88% of away-offense snaps), the rating edge is tuned to the training home margin (2.09 points, 2016-2023 without 2020 and neutral sites; tuned sim 2.04). Special teams are untouched beyond what ratings do. Tuning record and grid: data/game/league.json `tuning`; command `node --import ./scripts/engine/register.mjs scripts/game/tune.ts`.

**Team strength (designed, resolved question 6).** Each generated team's players all get round(1.2 x a standard normal draw) rating points (0-99 clamp). 1.2 is interpolated to the 2016-2023 margin SD (14.14); league-average teams already gave 13.99.
