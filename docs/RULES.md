# RULES.md

The NFL rules the game state machine implements (packages/sim/src/game). Every rule number lives in `data/rules/nfl-2026.json`; this file cites each one. Rules are looked up, never written from memory.

## Edition used

**2026 Official Playing Rules of the National Football League**, published by NFL Football Operations at https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook (the page links the full PDF, ops-2026-NFL-Rulebook-FINAL, hosted at https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/fl_attachment/league/tqivdkzt9mu6wdgsh1ku.pdf). The 2026 rulebook is published, so the fallback (2025 rulebook plus 2026 announcements) was not needed. Every value below was read in that rulebook on 2026-11-11. History for the kickoff rules also cites the 2025 rule-change announcement on operations.nfl.com.

## Unconfirmed values

None. Every leaf in the rule set was read in the 2026 rulebook. Rows marked `designed` are deliberate choices of the game, listed next.

## Designed values

These are the game's own choices, not NFL rules. Each also has an entry in docs/DECISIONS.md.

- `id`: the file's name for itself.
- `overtime.postseason.periodsPerCoinToss` and `overtime.postseason.timingAsPeriod`: the rulebook covers four postseason overtime periods and then says play continues until a winner. The game repeats the four-period cycle (new toss, the same timing pattern).
- `penalties.offensiveHolding.enforcement`, `penalties.defensiveHolding.enforcement`: previous spot on every play (the passing-play rule), not three-and-one on runs.
- `penalties.returnTeamIllegalBlock.enforcement`: always the spot of the foul.
- `penalties.unnecessaryRoughnessDeadBall.tenSecondRunoff`: no run-off for a dead-ball foul after the down.
- `penalties.unnecessaryRoughness.enforcement`, `penalties.unnecessaryRoughness.alternate.spot`, `penalties.unnecessaryRoughness.alternate.yards`: one entry for both teams. By the defense this is the rule (previous spot or dead-ball spot, whichever is better for the offense). By the offense the game uses the same two spots and gives the defense the better one, where the rule is three-and-one from the basic spot.

Mechanics the game simplifies without a rule-set value (DECISIONS.md, "M1 step 3"): whole-yard spots, no momentum exception, at most one foul enforced per play, fouls by both teams replay the down (two-team fouls with a change of possession or on a kick are rejected), no penalty yardage carried to the next try or kickoff, no fair catch kick, no injuries, no challenges or replay.

## How to add a rule

Add the value to the rule set file and a row to the table below: key path, value, the rule in plain words, rule and section, source URL, season, date checked, and status (`confirmed`, `unconfirmed` or `designed`). A test checks that every leaf has exactly one row and every row names a leaf. A `confirmed` row must cite operations.nfl.com or another nfl.com host that serves the official rulebook or NFL Football Operations announcements.

## Kickoffs

The dynamic kickoff, as adopted in 2024 and amended in 2025 and 2026.

- **2024 (adoption, history only, no value here depends on it).** Kick from the 35; landing zone from the receiving team's goal line to its 20; kick touching the landing zone then into the end zone, touchback at the 20; kick into the end zone in the air, downed or out the back, touchback at the 30; short of the landing zone or out of bounds, receivers at their 40; onside kick only in the fourth quarter while trailing, declared in advance. Source: nfl.com news, "2024 NFL Season: Explaining rules for new dynamic kickoff", https://www.nfl.com/news/2024-nfl-season-explaining-rules-for-new-dynamic-kickoff (checked 2026-11-11). That page is NFL-owned news, not the rulebook; it is cited for history only.
- **2025 amendments.** The kickoff was made permanent; touchback after a kick that lands in the end zone moved to the 35; setup zone alignment changed (at most three receivers not on the restraining line, at most one per area); onside kick allowed at any point while trailing, not only in the fourth quarter. Overtime: the regular season adopted the guaranteed possession with a 10-minute period. Source: NFL Football Operations, 2025 Rules Changes, https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/featured-rules (checked 2026-11-11).
- **2026 changes. Yes, 2026 changed kickoff mechanics from 2025.** The 2026 rule changes list on https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook names three kickoff changes: 6-1-3 modifies the receiving team's setup zone alignment (now at most four players not on the restraining line, never more than two per area, and at least six on the line when more than nine are in the zone); 6-1-5 makes every touchback after a kickoff from the 50-yard line (after a penalty) a touchback at the 20; 6-1-6 lets the kicking team declare an onside kick at any time during the game, not only when trailing. The alignment change has no field in the state machine; the other two are `kickoff.midfieldKickSpot`, `kickoff.touchbackFromMidfield` and `kickoff.onside.allowedWhen`.

Current values (2026): kick from the 35 (`kickoff.spot`), safety kick from the 20; landing zone 0 to 20; touchback at the 20 after the landing zone, at the 35 from the end zone, at the 20 for any kick from the 50; short or out of bounds, 25 yards from the spot of the kick (30 for a safety kick) or the landing or out-of-bounds spot if closer to the kicker; fair catch allowed at the spot of the catch; onside kick at any time, declared.

PlayResult outcomes map to Rule 6-1-5: `touchbackEndZone` is (b) to (d), `touchbackLandingZone` is (a).

## Scoring and tries

Touchdown 6, field goal 3, safety 2 (Rule 11-1-2). Tries from the 15 (kick) or the 2 (run or pass); 1 for a kick, 2 for a touchdown by either team, 1 to the opponent for what would be a safety (Rule 11-3). The team on defense during the try receives the next kickoff (Rule 11-3-4); after a field goal the team scored upon receives (Rule 11-4-6); after a safety the team scored upon free-kicks from its 20 (Rule 11-5-2).

Missed field goals (Rule 11-4-2): receivers' ball at the spot of the kick, or at their 20 if the kick was from inside it. PlayResult reports the spot of the kick.

A punt blocked behind the line and recovered by the kicking team, untouched by the receivers beyond the line, keeps the same series of downs: on fourth down short of the line to gain it is a turnover on downs (Rule 9-3-1, Item 1). Kickers recovering a punt the receivers touched beyond the line get a new series (Rule 9-3-2, Item 1, and 7-3-1 (d)); recovering one the receivers never touched beyond the line gives the receivers the ball there (Rule 9-3-2, Item 2).

## Clock

Out-of-bounds plays restart on the ready signal except after a change of possession, after the two-minute warning of the first half, and inside the last five minutes of the second half (Rule 4-3-2 (a)). Penalties restart the clock as if the foul had not occurred, with the same windows (Rule 4-3-2 (e)). The clock stops for an incomplete pass, out of bounds, a ball dead on or behind a goal line, a foul, a change of possession, the two-minute warning and the end of a free kick down (Rule 4-4). A fumble out of bounds restarts on the ready signal (Rule 4-3-2 (f)). Play clock 40 after a play, 25 after administrative stoppages, 30 after a run-off (Rule 4-6). The two-minute warning comes at the end of the last down snapped before 2:00 in the second and fourth periods (Rule 3-41), so during pre-snap run-off the clock stops at 2:00 exactly. A ten-second run-off follows an offensive act that conserves time after the warning; the offense may spend a timeout instead, and declining the penalty declines the run-off (Rule 4-7-1). After the warning, a defensive foul that stops a running clock between downs restarts the clock on the ready signal with a 40-second play clock, unless the offense chooses the snap (Rule 4-7-1, Item 2; Rule 4-6-3 (b)). A declined foul is timed like an accepted one: the clock starts as if it had not occurred, except on the snap inside the windows (Rule 4-3-2 (e), "following enforcement or declination"). An untimed down follows an accepted defensive foul on the last play of any period, or a double foul on the last play of a period that ends a half, never an offensive foul alone (Rule 4-8-2 (a), (b), (h)). A pre-snap foul on an untimed down does not use it up. A timeout spent instead of a run-off counts as that team's timeout for the dead-ball period (Rule 4-5-1, Item 3).

Not modeled: the defense spending a timeout instead of the clock starting after its time-conserving foul (Rule 4-7-1, Item 2), the defense declining only the run-off, Rule 4-7-3 (a defensive foul in the last 40 seconds ends the half unless the defense has a timeout), injury timeouts and their run-offs (Rule 4-5-2 to 4-5-4), excess timeouts.

## Overtime

### Regular season

One 10-minute period after a coin toss; both teams get an opportunity to possess, unless the kicking team scores a safety on the receiving team's first possession; after both have had it, the leader wins; level after that, the next score wins; tied at the end of the period, the game is a tie. Two timeouts each; fourth-quarter timing, including the two-minute warning (Rule 16-1-2, 16-1-3, 16-1-5).

### Postseason

15-minute periods until there is a winner, with the same possession rules; three timeouts per half of two periods; the third period opens with a kickoff as a second half does; the second period ends with first-half timing and the fourth with fourth-period timing; a new coin toss after the fourth period. A postseason game never ends tied (Rule 16-1-4, 16-1-5).

### Both

A kickoff is the receiving team's opportunity to possess; a kick it muffs counts as one (Rule 16-1-5 (c)). A defense that intercepts or recovers has possessed even if it loses the ball again on the same down (Rule 16-1-5 (b)); the PlayResult reports this with `defensePossessedDuringPlay`. The machine tracks whether the second team has had its opportunity; once it has and its possession has ended, the leader wins, and level means sudden death. A try is skipped when no result of it could change who wins (Rule 4-8-2 (c)).

## Timeouts

Three per half, one per team per dead-ball period, no carry-over (Rule 4-5-1). Timeouts may be called during any dead ball, including before a try.

## Challenges and replay review

Not modeled yet: challenges are deferred to M2 (docs/DECISIONS.md, "Challenges deferred to M2"), and the rule set file has no challenge or replay values. The rules below were read so M2 can build from them.

Source for every item in this section: 2026 Official Playing Rules of the National Football League, Rule 15 (Instant Replay) and Rule 16, https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook (PDF at https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/fl_attachment/league/tqivdkzt9mu6wdgsh1ku.pdf), season 2026, checked 2026-11-12. The 2026 rule-change list on that page has no replay or challenge change (its only officiating item, 19-2, lets League personnel consult on disqualifications for flagrant acts). The last replay change was the 2025 expansion of replay assist, listed under 2025 Rules Changes on https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/featured-rules (checked 2026-11-12).

**Coach's challenges (Rule 15, Section 1, Article 1).**

- Each team gets a "minimum of two challenges", started by the head coach (or a pregame designee) throwing the red flag before the next legal snap or kick (15-1-1 (a)).
- A third challenge is allowed only if at least one of the team's challenges succeeded. There is never a fourth (15-1-1 (f)).
- Each challenge needs an available timeout. A team out of timeouts or out of challenges may not challenge (15-1-1 (d)).
- An unsuccessful challenge costs a timeout (15-1-1 (e); 15-6-1). A team that takes a timeout and then loses a challenge is charged a second timeout (15-6-1). A challenge succeeds if any reviewable aspect of the play is changed (15-6-1); for a line-to-gain review it succeeds only if the first-down ruling changes, not just the spot (15-3-7, Notes 2 and 3).
- A team that commits a foul preventing the next snap loses the right to challenge the previous play; the other team keeps it (15-1-1 (b)).
- Coaches may challenge the rulings listed in Section 3 except plays only the Replay Official can review (15-1-1 (c)). Challenging when not permitted (which covers a play reserved to the Replay Official) charges a team timeout (15-1-1 (d)).
- Challenging with no timeouts left: loss of 15 yards, enforced as a foul between downs (15-1-1 (d), Penalty). It is never part of a multiple foul and is enforced in addition to any other foul (Rule 14, Section 1, Article 3, Exception 1; also 14-1-2, Item 4, Exception).
- Overtime: no coach's challenges in any overtime period; the Replay Official initiates every review (Rule 16, Section 1, Article 5 (d)).

**Reviews the Replay Official starts (Rule 15, Section 1, Article 2).** Only the Replay Official or the Senior Vice President of Officiating (or designee) may start a review of: (a) any play that begins after the two-minute warning of each half; (b) any play in overtime; (c) any scoring play; (d) every try, good or not; (e) on-field rulings of an interception, a fumble or backward pass recovered by the opponent or out of bounds through the opponent's end zone, a failure to reach the line to gain on fourth down, kicking-team possession at the end of a free or scrimmage kick, or a player's disqualification. These can be reviewed even when an accepted foul would wipe out the ruling. The review must start before the next legal snap or kick. A ball touching a foreign object (scoreboard, guide wire) can be reviewed by the Replay Official at any time (15-3-11, Item 5, Note 1).

**Standard for reversal (Rule 15, Section 2, Article 1).** A ruling changes only when the League office reviewer finds "clear and obvious video evidence" that warrants it. Once a review starts, every reviewable aspect of the play can change, not only the one challenged (15-2-2). Reviews are run by the Senior Vice President of Officiating or designee with the Replay Official and Referee; an expedited review can skip the Referee (15-2).

**What can be reviewed (Rule 15, Section 3).** Possession (catch, recovery, pass or fumble, fumble before down), touching of the ball or ground, goal-line plays, boundary-line plays, line-of-scrimmage plays (illegal pass, illegal kick), the dead-ball spot against the line to gain, players on the field, game administration (penalty enforcement, proper down, spot of a foul, game clock), disqualification, and others (pass direction, illegal forward pass or handoff, field goals that cross below the top of the uprights or touch something, ball hitting a foreign object). The game clock can be reviewed to restore time, never to take it off (15-3-9).

**Not reviewable (Rule 15, Section 4).** An erroneous whistle; illegal batting or kicking; intentional grounding; ineligible receiver downfield; illegal contact on a receiver; illegal blocks; anything not listed in Section 3.

**After a review.** A changed ruling resets the game clock to when the ball should have been dead (15-5-1). After the two-minute warning, a changed ruling that leaves the clock running brings a 10-second run-off (play clock 30) that neither team may decline but either may avoid with a timeout (15-5-2). A timeout a team took after the two-minute warning is given back if the review changes the ruling (15-6-2). Pre-snap fouls (other than personal fouls and unsportsmanlike conduct) committed before the review are ignored if the ruling changes, and a coach may change an accept-or-decline choice made before the review (15-7-3, Items 2 and 3).

**Replay assist (Rule 15, Section 9; added 2025).** The Replay Official or the officiating department may advise the on-field crew on objective aspects of a play when there is clear and obvious video evidence, and on game administration: penalty enforcement, proper down, timing or spot of a foul, the game clock, possession, whether a pass was complete, intercepted or its direction, touching of a loose ball or a line, ball or player position against a line or the pocket, down by contact (only when not ruled down on the field). It can also remove certain called fouls when video shows the needed contact did not happen: roughing the passer for a hit to the head or neck, intentional grounding with the passer out of the pocket, unnecessary roughness on a runner out of bounds or a hit to a defenseless player's head or neck, facemask, horse-collar, tripping, and roughing or running into the kicker when the defender touched the kick. Assist does not replace a challenge or a Replay Official review.

## Penalties

The table holds enough real penalties to exercise every enforcement mechanic, plus the fouls the play engine emits most often (M1 step 4 added offensive pass interference, illegal contact and live-ball unnecessary roughness, cited below from the 2026 rulebook text on operations.nfl.com). Real fouls outside the table are not emitted; docs/ENGINE_REPORT.md reports their rate and yards. Fouls whose yardage would carry to the next try or kickoff (Rule 14-2-3, 11-3-3, 14-4-9 Exc.) are not supported: the offended team chooses between the play's result and customary enforcement.

**When the passer's protection applies (M1 step 5c, G2).** The special roughing-the-passer rules cover "a player who is in a passing posture (i.e. before, during, or after throwing or attempting a pass)" (Rule 12, Section 2, Article 11), and they "apply on all passes (legal or illegal) thrown from behind the line of scrimmage" (Rule 8, Section 1, Article 2, Note 2); against a passer who throws from beyond the line, unnecessary roughness may apply instead. A quarterback who scrambles and runs without throwing never attempts a pass on that down, so he is a runner, not a passer: roughing the passer is not a foul the defense can commit on a scramble. Source: 2026 Official Playing Rules of the National Football League, https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook (PDF at https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/fl_attachment/league/tqivdkzt9mu6wdgsh1ku.pdf), pages 37 and 55-56, season 2026, checked 2026-11-12. No rule set value changes: the engine stops drawing the foul on scrambles (docs/DECISIONS.md, M1 step 5c, G2).
